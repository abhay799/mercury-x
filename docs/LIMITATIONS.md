# MERCURY X Limitations

MERCURY X is research and engineering portfolio software. Its strongest implemented capabilities are in typed control-plane modeling, deterministic decision logic, invariant validation, provenance, lifecycle handling, adversarial tests, and executable internal certification gates.

## Implemented Control-Plane Behavior

The repository implements and tests:

- canonical request intake and workload intelligence;
- logical execution-graph construction and validation;
- model capability, composition, precision, and morphing contracts;
- session and governed global-context memory;
- context prediction, semantic KV compatibility, and disaggregated execution plans;
- hardware personality and topology representations;
- placement prediction and bounded speculative execution;
- reasoning budgets, quality-aware scheduling, intelligence SLO compilation, and compute negotiation;
- migration and self-healing state machines with verification boundaries;
- adversarial scheduler challenges and counterfactual simulations;
- controlled policy evolution with approval and rollback states;
- federation and privacy control artifacts;
- digital-twin and supervisory control abstractions;
- explicit production/research platform modes;
- per-phase internal certification and repository regression tests.

These capabilities demonstrate control logic. They do not, by themselves, operate external infrastructure.

## Not Yet Claimed / Requires Real Infrastructure

### Datacenter autonomy

There is no claim that MERCURY X autonomously operates a real datacenter. Production operation would require resource managers, orchestration, identity systems, observability pipelines, incident controls, operators, and extensive safety evaluation.

### GPU and accelerator execution

The local baseline is CPU-first. Hardware contracts and placement decisions do not prove kernel execution, driver integration, device telemetry, accelerator isolation, or measured GPU behavior.

### Live GPU-memory migration

Phase 21 implements migration control contracts and lifecycle logic. It does not establish production-grade transfer of live GPU memory, KV state, device context, or in-flight kernels across physical machines.

### Multi-datacenter federation

Federation identities, capabilities, topology snapshots, and placement decisions are control-plane artifacts. Real federation requires authenticated cross-domain transport, trust management, capacity integration, network failure handling, and operational governance.

### Hardware-backed privacy

Privacy envelopes express classifications, purposes, residency, and policy constraints. They do not prove confidential-computing hardware, remote attestation, encrypted memory, key custody, or regulatory compliance.

### Calibrated thermal and power twin

The datacenter twin supports scenarios and calibration state. No claim is made that it accurately predicts a real facility’s thermal, power, cooling, network, or failure dynamics without explicit measured calibration evidence.

### Trained autonomous scheduler

Deterministic and pluggable backend interfaces do not imply a trained, validated autonomous scheduler. No learned policy should be claimed without a concrete versioned artifact, training data lineage, evaluation, calibration, and operating-domain evidence.

### Hyperscale performance

The repository does not claim measured hyperscale throughput, tail latency, cost savings, utilization, energy reduction, quality improvement, or fault-recovery performance. Test counts are software verification results, not performance benchmarks.

### Large-scale production traffic

No claim is made that the system has handled real production traffic, adversarial internet exposure, long-duration operation, regulatory audits, or production incident response.

## Simulation and Evidence Boundary

Counterfactual, adversarial, and digital-twin outputs are advisory. Synthetic fixtures test logical behavior. Neither category should be reported as measured infrastructure evidence. Empirical claims require a documented environment, method, artifact version, dataset lineage, uncertainty treatment, and reproducible result.

## Security and Compliance Boundary

Fail-closed authorization and privacy invariants are architecture features, not a completed threat model or external security assessment. The repository does not claim compliance with a legal or industry framework. Deployments would require independent review, secrets management, identity integration, supply-chain controls, logging policy, data governance, and penetration testing.

## Productization Work Remaining

Future work is productization, not a new numbered phase. It includes:

- real infrastructure and cloud adapters;
- empirical hardware, network, power, and thermal benchmarks;
- calibrated simulation models;
- operator and experiment user interfaces;
- deployment packaging and operational runbooks;
- security and privacy validation on target infrastructure;
- reproducible large-scale experiments;
- external review of safety and governance controls.
